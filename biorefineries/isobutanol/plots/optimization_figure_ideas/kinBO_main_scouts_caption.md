**Figure N | Isobutanol TRY campaigns scout profitable designs that a profitability-only search rarely visits; seeded with their trials, the profitability search finds a profitable ethanol + isobutanol co-producer.** Eight Gaussian-process (GP) campaigns searched one 12-dimensional strain-design and feeding space (random seed 350). Two maximized profitability (profitability index; shown as internal rate of return, IRR): **uninformed** (cyan, 2,000 trials) and **TRY-informed** (teal, 1,000 trials; GP preloaded with 1,000 scout trials: the 327 at least as profitable as the starting strain plus 673 space-filling ones). Six scouts each maximized one titer, rate or yield of ethanol (amber) or isobutanol (violet). Dashed cyan: uninformed plateau, 15.8 % (tinted above); dotted grey: starting strain, 12.6 %; dotted violet: best seed, 22.9 %; loss: IRR < 0 or none. Making isobutanol: ≥ 5 g·L⁻¹; co-production: ≥ 5 g·L⁻¹ of each alcohol.

**(a)** Best IRR so far. The uninformed campaign plateaued at 15.8 % (ethanol only) from trial 104 to 2,000; cyan strip: its space-filling start-up, shared with the scouts. The TRY-informed campaign had none: its GP proposed every trial. Left: the seeds by scout family; all 112 above 15.8 % are from isobutanol scouts. After a first trial that lost money, the TRY-informed campaign co-produced at 19.8 % in its second and beat every seed in its 13th (23.8 %). Inset: refinement to 27.3 % at trial 913 (PI 0.73 → 0.81, seven times the reproducibility margin).

**(b)** IRR vs isobutanol's share of the alcohol titer for all 13,611 designs making ≥ 1 g·L⁻¹ alcohol (ethanol-scout designs lie mostly under the uninformed column). No design making < 5 g·L⁻¹ isobutanol exceeds 15.8 %. The uninformed campaign's 97 isobutanol-making designs (outlined; 95 proposed after trial 104, 5 % of its later trials) were all worse (best 13.9 %) and lost money more often (64 % vs 33 %). All 296 designs above 22.9 % are TRY-informed co-producers (★: 41 g·L⁻¹ ethanol + 40 g·L⁻¹ isobutanol).

**(c)** Each campaign's trials (strip), highest-IRR visit (open) and returned design, the argmax of its own objective (filled). The isobutanol scouts visited 110, 1 and 1 designs above 15.8 % but returned 1.7 % and two with no IRR; the ethanol scouts never exceeded 15.4 % and returned 8.1–12.7 %.

**(d, e)** Titers and metabolic proteome of each returned design (Pdc and ALS→Aro10 commit carbon to ethanol and isobutanol; light shades: Adh1, Adh6; constant sectors omitted). Dotted: the penalty-free budget, 0.135 g·(g DCW)⁻¹; the four designs beyond it grow more slowly (×0.84, ×0.63, ×0.30, ×0.997). Here neither objective alone reliably returned a profitable co-producer: the isobutanol TRY campaigns visited such designs but returned low-IRR ones, the ethanol TRY campaigns stayed below the plateau, and unseeded profitability search plateaued on ethanol in one of two runs. Seeded with the scouts' trials, profitability search beat every seed within 13 trials and reached 25 % within 19 trials in both seeded runs (Fig. S1).

*Methods notes (not part of the legend).*
* Every campaign starts from scenario A and is subject to the enzyme-burden constraint; titers are per litre of water. The TRY-informed campaign's preload (1,000 trials) exceeded its start-up count, so it had no space-filling start-up.
* PI = net present value at a 15 % hurdle rate / total capital investment. The reproducibility margin is the cross-process margin of the deterministic PI objective, ΔPI 0.0105 (about 0.2 IRR points); smaller differences are not interpreted.
* The seeds were preloaded with their recorded objective values, without re-simulation. The scouts' 11,968 simulations are by-products of the TRY campaigns and are not counted on the trial axes.
* The best design lies at five search bounds, so higher IRR may exist outside the searched ranges.
* IRRs are at default prices under the model version used for the campaigns (starting strain 12.6 %; 12.8 % under the current model version).
